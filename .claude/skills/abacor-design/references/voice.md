# Voice

Abacor talks to accountants. They are busy, precise, and sceptical of software
that overclaims. Write like a competent colleague, not like marketing.

---

## Rules

**Plain sentences.** Short. Let the fact do the work.

**No em dashes.** Use a comma, a full stop, or restructure.

**Say what happens, not what it enables.** "Abacor reads your mailbox and never
sends, replies or files anything" beats "seamlessly integrate your inbox".

**Never promise what the product does not do.** A line claiming a 24-hour undo
that does not exist will be caught in review and costs more trust than the
feature was worth.

**Point at real places.** "Listed under Excluded senders in Integrations" beats
"review them any time". If the copy implies a destination, that destination must
exist.

**Numbers over adjectives.** "8,412 emails across 184 people" beats
"a comprehensive scan".

**Sentence case everywhere** except the 11px section labels, which are uppercase
with 4% tracking.

---

## Terminology

Use the word the user uses, and use it consistently.

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| emails | messages, mail, correspondence |
| contacts | people, records, entries |
| inbox | mailbox, email account |
| import | sync, ingest, pull |
| firm | organisation, company, workspace |

One inconsistency worth knowing: **sync** is correct for the ongoing automatic
process, **import** for the one-time action. "Import your emails, then Abacor
syncs new ones hourly."

---

## Buttons

Name the outcome, not the mechanism.

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Import emails | Continue |
| Choose contacts | Next |
| Review 16 contacts | Proceed |
| Import and sync | Confirm |
| Back to Abacor | Done |
| Invite your team | Finish |

A button that says Next tells the user nothing about what they are agreeing to.
Where the count is known, put it in the label.

---

## Helper text

The footer line answers the question the user has at that exact moment.

- Before a scan: what will be read, and for how far back.
- During: that closing is safe, and where to return.
- Before writing: that nothing is saved yet.
- After: what changed and what happens next.

Never repeat the headline in the helper. If the headline says "Preview ready",
the helper says what was found, not that the preview is ready.

---

## Confirmations

Structure, in order:

1. What happened, as a fact. "Your emails are in Abacor."
2. The numbers. "1,284 emails are now on 16 contact timelines."
3. What changes going forward. "Abacor imports new emails hourly."
4. One way out.

Do not re-sell the feature after the user has already used it.

---

## Empty states

One line. "No matches found." No suggestions, no illustration, no apology.

---

## Things to delete on sight

- "Seamlessly", "effortlessly", "powerful", "robust", "leverage", "unlock"
  (except literally unlocking a plan limit)
- "We're excited to..."
- Exclamation marks
- "Simply" and "just" before an instruction
- Any sentence that could be cut without losing a fact
